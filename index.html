                            Methods and QC

                            Which stations, and why

                            • In the basin. Sagehen Creek above the USGS gauge drains 27.7 km² from 6,330 ft at the gauge to about 8,740 ft on the crest. Three instruments sit in it or on its edge: the field station's East Meadow tower beside the gauge (6,337 ft; a Geonor weighing precipitation gauge plus temperature, humidity, wind and radiation at 25 and 100 ft, run by the UC Natural Reserve System), Independence Lake SNOTEL on the west ridge (541, 8,340 ft, the only snow pillow in the basin) and the USGS gauge itself.
                            • Nearby. Independence Camp (539, 6,970 ft) and Independence Creek (540, 6,440 ft) SNOTEL sites, 3 to 5 miles north; the USGS precipitation gauge at Independence Creek (392724120171001, about 6,920 ft, beside Independence Camp, 15 min totals, not on MesoWest; its temperature here is Independence Camp's); and the NV Energy gauge at Hobart Mills (6,030 ft, 3 miles southeast).
                            • Context. RAWS at Rice Canyon, Tahoe Donner and Stampede, and SNOTEL at the Central Sierra Snow Lab (Donner Summit) and Truckee #2. RAWS buckets are unheated.
                            • Streamflow: USGS 10343500 Sagehen Creek near Truckee, a Hydrologic Benchmark station with a record since 1953. Discharge, stage and water temperature every 15 min, provisional.
                            • Radar: amounts from NCEP Stage IV (radar adjusted to gauges, hourly) for windows up to 8 days and NOAA MRMS daily totals beyond, both from the IEM IEMRE point service at the basin center (39.432, -120.272). The NEXRAD mosaic (IEM, 5 min) is on the map and in the "radar now" tile. The Reno and Sacramento radars see the basin over the crest, so low snow is often missed.

                            Weighing gauge (field station)

                            • The UC NRS Dendra feed still carries the gauge's raw vibrating wire frequency every 10 min, although its published millimetre series stopped in July 2020. From October 2017 to July 2020 the published bucket content was exactly content = 9.0948×10⁻⁵ f² − 0.028877 f − 73.951 mm (135,169 pairs, residual 0.09 mm, the same each year), the logger's own calibration. The page applies it to today's frequency.
                            • Content is the median of the last hour (7 readings); precipitation counts when it rises 0.25 mm above the last level. A drop of more than 1 mm held for an hour (evaporation, servicing) or more than 15 mm at once (a drain: 10/2 and 12/23/2025, 2/17 and 7/13/2026) resets the level and counts nothing.
                            • Frequencies outside 500 to 3,500 Hz are missing. Readings that span more than 8 mm within 30 min while swinging both up and down by more than 4 mm are a fault (August and September 2026, when the record jumped by tens of mm; left in, it added 485 mm of false rain in August). A fault lasting 2 h or more also drops the 3 h either side.
                            • Checked against SNOTEL for 3 Oct 2025 to 31 Jul 2026: 889 mm here, 790 mm at Independence Camp and 1,199 mm at Independence Lake; two day totals correlate at r = 0.95 and 0.98. False precipitation over the dry spells of the year: 1.6 mm. It missed the 9/3 to 9/4/2026 storm while faulting.

                            Other gauges

                            • SNOTEL storage gauges report hourly in 0.1 in (2.54 mm) steps and swing with temperature, by up to 10 mm over a day (Independence Creek, October 2026). Water that falls stays in the gauge and a thermal swing comes back down, so each gauge is read through its lowest value over the following 24 h and only new highs of that count. A rise less than 24 h old, or a single 0.1 in step of any age (slow drift: four sites each gained one step from 10/11 to 10/13/2026 with radar dry), counts only if the field station gauge, another gauge within 5 miles (not a SNOTEL) or Stage IV radar saw 0.5 mm or more within 3 h. A drop of more than 25 mm resets the high (the October season reset).
                            • RAWS (Rice Canyon, Tahoe Donner, Stampede): first differences of cumulative precip_accum; negative steps count as 0; steps that span more than 30 min are flagged. Hobart Mills: sum of 10 min accumulations. Increments faster than 10 mm per 10 min are left out unless another gauge recorded precipitation within 30 min.
                            • Unheated tipping buckets (RAWS and Hobart Mills) hold snow and tip when it melts. Their tips are marked possible melt, kept in their own totals and left out of storm detection, until their air has stayed at or above 1 °C for 24 h.
                            • "No data" (no reports) is distinct from 0. A total is partial when its last report is more than 25 min before the end of the interval (more than 70 min for the hourly SNOTEL gauges).

                            Rain line

                            • Each hour, a straight line is fit to air temperature against elevation for every station reporting (6,030 to 8,340 ft, ten stations); the rain line is where that line crosses 1 °C (34 °F). It is left blank when the fit is poor (r² < 0.6) or temperature rises with height (an inversion, common in the Little Truckee basin on clear nights). Above about 9,500 ft it is an extrapolation beyond the highest station. Not yet checked against soundings here (at Alta the same method ran about 470 ft below the free air value while precipitating).

                            Storms and response

                            • Increments from the detection gauges (default: the field station gauge and Independence Lake and Camp SNOTEL) are pooled in time order; a gap over 3 h starts a new group. A group is a storm when any detection gauge totals at least 1.0 mm.
                            • Rain or snow: precipitation weighted mean of each gauge's own air temperature (the tower's for the field station gauge); below 1 °C (34 °F) is called mixed or snow.
                            • Flow response at the USGS gauge: baseline is the time weighted mean of the 6 h before the start; peak is the maximum up to 48 h after the end, cut at the next storm. Lag runs to the peak from the centroid of the basin precipitation that fell before it. A clear response needs a rise of at least 3 gauge steps and 5% of the baseline; a peak before the centroid, or more than 12 h after it, gets no lag. From November to March, after a below freezing day at the tower, flow is marked ice possible (USGS: ice backs water up and raises stage with no change in flow; USGS edits these periods later).
                            • Flow QC: a report far from the median of its five neighbors on each side is removed; a wild newest report is held until the next one confirms it.

                            Cameras: greenness and meadow snow

                            • The PhenoCam Network publishes a daily summary for a fixed patch (region of interest) of each Sagehen camera: GCC, the green chromatic coordinate (green over red plus green plus blue), and the patch's mean red, green and blue. The page shows their 90th percentile GCC and leaves out days they flag as outliers. Their files can't be read from a browser, so the Alta Hydromet Action copies them to its data branch; PhenoCam itself runs a few days behind.
                            • Their snow flag is empty for these cameras, so snow is called here from the blue share of the meadow patches, blue over red plus green plus blue, averaged over the two meadow cameras: 0.32 or more is snow. Against Independence Creek SNOTEL depth (1 in or more, 2 miles north, 6,440 ft), 2017 to 2026, the call agreed on 96% of 3,218 days; most disagreements are fall dustings and the open meadow melting out earlier or later than the SNOTEL site.

                            Snow depth QC

                            • SNOTEL depth comes from AWDB hourly data for windows up to 45 days (stamps are Pacific Standard Time) and daily data for longer windows. The Tahoe Donner RAWS sonic depth is shown after QC: values outside 0 to 4,000 mm are dropped, a nonzero depth unchanged for 48 h is a stuck sensor, and readings of 150 mm or less within 3 h of a 0 are bare ground noise. The tower's own depth sensor has sent nothing since August 2025.
